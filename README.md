**Chada Tech - Sprint Review and Retrospective **

Author: Faiz Farid | Southern New Hampshire University

Project Summary

This repository holds my final deliverable for CS 250, the Sprint Review and Retrospective for the SNHU Travel Scrum-Agile pilot at ChadaTech. Across the course I worked through each Scrum role (Scrum Master, Product Owner, Tester, and Developer) while a niche vacation booking tool took shape, from user stories and test cases to a working Java slideshow. The retrospective pulls those experiences together and evaluates whether Scrum-Agile was the right approach.

Reflection
How do I interpret user needs and implement them into a program? How does creating "user stories" help with this?

I start by listening to the people who will use the product. In the SNHU Travel project, a customer focus group told us they wanted personalized destination lists, price limits, and a choice of vacation type, and I turned that feedback into user stories written as "As a [user], I want [goal], so that [reason]." The "so that" part keeps the real user's need in front of the team, so we build what someone actually asked for instead of what we assume. Acceptance criteria then make each story testable, and sizing stories as small, medium, or large helped us decide what to build first. When a story was unclear, asking the Product Owner a specific question early worked far better than guessing.

How do I approach developing programs? What Agile processes do I hope to incorporate into my future development work?

I like to build in small, working pieces, test as I go, and adjust when requirements change. This project showed me why: when the focus moved from a general destination list to a slideshow and then to wellness travel, short sprints meant I only had to change images and text instead of starting over. In my future work I hope to use Daily Scrums to surface blockers early, Backlog Refinement to write clear acceptance criteria before coding starts, and a Sprint Retrospective that ends with one real change for the next sprint. I also want to use a digital board such as Jira or Azure Boards so everyone can see the status of the work.

What does it mean to be a good team member in software development?

A good team member communicates clearly, takes ownership of their part, and makes problems visible early instead of hiding them. Playing every role taught me what each person needs from the others: testers need clear acceptance criteria, developers need stable priorities, and a Product Owner needs to stay available to answer questions. It also means asking specific questions of the right person, being open to feedback, and sharing knowledge so no one becomes a single point of failure.
